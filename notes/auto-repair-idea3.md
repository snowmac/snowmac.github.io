# Idea 3: Auto Repair — plus why fab shops/machine shops don't work as a search vertical

Status: research phase, explored via Semrush MCP on 2026-09-14. Came out of checking
industrial/trade-adjacent verticals (fab shops, machine shops, welding, CNC, auto/diesel/truck
repair) after the fab-shop case study (silverengineeringworks.com migration, see
`ai-consulting-seo-strategy.md`) raised the question of whether industrial is a repeatable
vertical, not just a one-off case study.

## Fab shops / machine shops / welding / CNC / industrial supply: not a search-driven vertical

Every term checked was tiny — this segment simply isn't discoverable via Google search, on
either the consumer or the "help me market my shop" side:

- metal fabrication denver: 170/mo | machine shop denver: 110/mo | welding shop denver: 20/mo
- cnc machining denver: 50/mo | sheet metal fabrication denver: 20/mo | industrial supply
  denver: 20/mo | precision machining denver: 20/mo

These businesses run on referral, word-of-mouth, trade shows, and B2B RFQ portals (ThomasNet)
instead — which matches how the actual Silver Engineering Works case study came about (a direct
personal relationship, not organic search). **Conclusion: industrial fab/machine shop work is
still a great one-off case study (proof-of-work content once the new site ships), but it is not
a repeatable inbound-acquisition vertical** the way med spa or chiropractic is — there's no
search demand to build a content/SEO strategy around, on either side of the transaction.

One adjacent term worth flagging and ruling out separately: "manufacturing marketing agency"
(1,900/mo, CPC $26.47) looks strong at a glance, but checking who ranks for it
(elevationb2b.com, altitudemarketing.com, kunocreative.com, themotionagency.com) confirms it's
an enterprise B2B/industrial-technology marketing category — account-based marketing for
mid-to-large manufacturers, not local small-shop owners. Different buyer, different sales
motion, not a fit for the local-retainer model this whole strategy is built on. Ruled out.

## Auto repair: a real, separate vertical worth considering

Unlike fab/machine shops, auto repair is consumer-facing and behaves like the other viable
verticals (med spa, chiropractic) — real local search volume and real "help me market my shop"
search behavior.

### Local consumer demand (Denver metro, Semrush US database, 2026-09-14)
- auto repair denver: 880/mo (CPC $4.82) | auto body shop denver: 260/mo | diesel repair
  denver: 90/mo | truck repair denver: 90/mo

### What auto shop owners search for help with
- auto repair shop marketing: 720/mo (CPC $13.99) — real, sizable signal
- auto shop seo: 110/mo, competitive density only **0.06** — wide open
- auto repair shop website design: 140/mo (CPC $12.57) | auto repair lead generation: 110/mo
  (CPC $17.88)
- auto repair marketing agency: only 40/mo — smaller than the med spa/chiro equivalent
  ("med spa marketing agency" 1,000/mo, "chiropractic marketing agency" 590/mo) — owners search
  more by "marketing" broadly than by "marketing agency" specifically in this vertical
- **Avoid:** auto repair shop software (880/mo, CPC $11.54) and auto repair shop crm (170/mo,
  CPC $16.17) — entrenched SaaS category (Tekmetric, Shopmonkey, Mitchell1); don't compete here,
  same rule as med spa's Boulevard/Zenoti and chiropractic's ChiroTouch

### AI-answering angle: unclaimed, same as med spa
- auto repair answering service: **0 volume** — nobody's searching for this specifically yet,
  same open-field pattern as med spa (unlike chiropractic, which already has 3+ AI-native
  competitors on "chiropractic answering service")
- ai for auto repair shops: 10/mo, small but present and rising

## Warm contact: local shop, 2 front-office people

Have a personal contact at a local shop. Initial read was that 2 front-office staff weakens the
"missed call = missed revenue" pitch (unlike a solo operator, they have coverage during business
hours). But clarified: the 2 front-office people are **service techs doubling as phone coverage**,
not dedicated receptionists — meaning the same "pulled away under a car, can't answer" pain point
still likely applies, just distributed across two people instead of one. Restores the core pitch;
don't assume it's solved just because headcount is 2.

**Still don't assume the pain point — ask.** Don't lead with "you need AI because you're
understaffed" (untrue, and comes off presumptuous). A warm, curious message asking what's
actually annoying about their front-office workflow (same low-pressure approach as the Josh
Fern outreach — market research from a friend, not a pitch) is the right first move before
assuming it's phones specifically. Could turn out to be no-shows, review generation, or online
booking instead, any of which still fits the same "marketing + AI-assisted front desk" offer
shape used for med spa.

Auto repair sits below med spa and chiropractic on ticket size/CPC (owners appear to have less
budget to spend per lead than med spa or chiropractic practices), but shares the same open field
on the AI-answering angle that made med spa attractive, and the "auto repair shop marketing"
volume (720/mo) is real and healthy. Reasonable #4 candidate if either of the top two doesn't
pan out, or as a second/parallel vertical once the playbook (site + AI answering + local SEO)
is proven on one of the higher-ticket verticals first.
